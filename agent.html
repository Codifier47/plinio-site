User. Me.

My work authorization was frozen while a federal court decided its fate, and the only public record was a docket I kept refreshing by hand. So I built an autonomous agent with Claude to do the watching. Every hour it loads the page, spots anything new, asks Claude what the change means for my case, and emails me in plain language.

What actually landed in my inbox, subject line and all, written by Claude:

docket-watch@ · Dorcas v. USCIS New entry
Subject
Final judgment entered: the hold on your case is vacated.
Verdict · this helps you

The court just entered final judgment against USCIS and set a compliance deadline. In plain terms: the policy freezing your work-authorization application has been struck down, and the agency is now ordered to resume adjudicating cases like yours.

This is the opening you've been waiting for. With the hold lifted, paying for premium processing now would start a hard 15-day clock. Worth acting on today.

detected 14:02 · diffed against 27 known entries · explained by claude
It told me the exact moment to pay for premium processing. My authorization came through about ten days later.
What the visibility bought me
The loop it runs

Scrape to inbox, every hour.

01
Scrape
A headless browser renders the live docket page and grabs the timeline, which only exists after JavaScript draws it.
02
Diff
It fingerprints every entry by hashing its date and text, then subtracts what it has already seen, so only genuinely new items get through.
03
Explain
Claude reads each new entry against the full context of my case and says, in plain sentences, whether it helps, hurts, or changes nothing.
04
Notify
The explanation hits my inbox at once, with a short daily heartbeat email when all is quiet, so silence never means the watchdog broke.

Over two weeks it caught more than twenty docket entries and flagged every move that mattered: the vacated hold, an enforcement motion, a final judgment against USCIS, the government's emergency stay and notice of appeal. Each arrived already explained for my situation. That visibility told me when to pay for premium processing, which puts the government on a strict clock. My work authorization was granted about ten days later.

The parts were ordinary: a browser, a hash, a scheduled job, an API call. Pointing them at something that actually mattered was the whole trick.
